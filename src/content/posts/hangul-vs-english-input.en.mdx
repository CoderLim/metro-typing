Metro Typing can accept station names in Korean or English depending on the mode you select in the original game. Mixing both in one rushed session usually hurts more than it helps.

## Practice Hangul when…

- You want real Seoul commute spelling under your fingers
- You are warming up for Korean typing work or study
- You already know the line order and need finger accuracy

Keep autocomplete under control. Wrong suggestions break rhythm faster than a slow correct keystroke. See [tips](/tips) for input hygiene.

## Practice English when…

- You are still learning Hangul and want the map feedback without IME friction
- You want to compare romanized names you see on tourist maps
- You are drilling one line’s English spellings before switching back to Hangul later

## Keep one session consistent

Pick one input style for a full set of runs. Switching mid-line forces two muscle maps at once. After a clean Hangul week on a short stretch, try the same stretch in English (or the reverse) as a deliberate second skill — not as a panic fallback when you mistype.

## Make the choice before the timer starts

Use a short setup check: type “Seoul Station,” “City Hall,” and “Gangnam” in the selected style before choosing a route. In Hangul mode, confirm the Korean input method is active. In English mode, glance at the spelling convention the game uses. This takes less than a minute and prevents a mode mismatch from being mistaken for a typing problem.

## A two-day comparison drill

On day one, type a five-station Line 2 section in Hangul twice and record corrections, not just time. On day two, use the same direction and section in English twice. Do not compare the raw times as if they measure one skill: compare which form creates more pauses and which station names require conscious spelling. The result tells you what to train next.

## Avoid switching as a rescue move

If you hesitate at Dongdaemun History & Culture Park in English or 동대문역사문화공원 in Hangul, finish the run in the chosen script. Switching because one name feels difficult teaches your hands that the setting is negotiable under pressure. Save the alternate script for a separate, clearly labeled session.

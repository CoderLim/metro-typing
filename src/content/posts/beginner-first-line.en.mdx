Your first Metro Typing line should teach the loop — pick, type, review — without turning into an endurance test. A long circular route looks impressive, but it hides where you actually struggle.

## Prefer a line you already ride

Commute memory is free practice data. Home station, office station, and one or two transfer anchors give your hands something to prepare before each name appears. If you do not live in Seoul, pick any short stretch you can finish twice in a row without rage-quitting.

## Short section before full line

When the game lets you set departure and arrival, start narrow. A short section means fewer sticky stations and cleaner feedback on the result screen. After two clean finishes, widen the interval or switch to a longer route.

## One direction only

Upbound and downbound reshuffle the order. For the first week, keep one direction so finish times are comparable. Switching sides too early makes every “new personal best” hard to trust.

## When to move up

Move to longer challenges such as Line 1 or the Line 2 loop only after the warm-up stretch feels automatic. That is when [tips for sticky stations](/tips) matter more than raw line length.

## A concrete first-session drill

Try a five-station section such as Jamsil to Garak Market on Line 8, or City Hall to Euljiro 3-ga on Line 2. Before the first run, say the start and end stations aloud. Run it once slowly, write down the station where you looked away or reached for Backspace, then repeat exactly the same direction. The second run is useful even when it is not faster: it tells you whether the delay came from route recall or finger input.

## Measure a baseline, not a lucky time

Keep a small note with the line, direction, device, completion time, and typo count. Three runs are enough for a beginner baseline. Use the middle time as your reference instead of the single fastest result, especially when one run includes a long pause at a station such as Seokchon or Songpa. A baseline makes the next week’s improvement visible.

## Signs your section is ready to grow

Add two or three stations only when you can finish the current section twice with no repeated station error and similar times. If the added station creates a pause, keep it as the last station for several runs before extending again. This keeps each upgrade specific: you are learning one new transition, not turning the whole route into a guess.
